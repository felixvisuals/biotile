# Print & cast guide (stage 1: classroom, clay)

## 0 · Measure your clay shrinkage first

1. Roll a test bar, scratch two marks exactly 100 mm apart.
2. Dry and fire it like the tiles. Measure the distance `L` in mm.
3. Shrinkage `= (100 − L)` %. Enter it in the editor (*Clay shrinkage*). 10 % is only a placeholder.

At 10 % the matrix is 176.7 mm wide, which fits the Bambu Lab A1 mini (180 mm). Above about 11 %,
switch to the P1S: the editor checks this.

## 1 · Print

| Part | Material | Settings |
|---|---|---|
| `matrix` | PLA (clay) / PETG, rPETG (concrete) | **0.08-0.12 mm layers** (layer lines would add a horizontal anisotropy), 3 walls, 15 % infill |
| `frame_*` (4×) | PLA / PETG | 0.2 mm, 4 walls; top/bottom walls may need diagonal placement |
| `backplate` | PLA | 0.2 mm, guide bosses up |
| `hole_punch` | PETG | 0.12 mm, 100 % infill |

All parts are already in print orientation in the ZIP (`stl/`, `3mf/`).

## 2 · Press moulding (clay)

1. Dust the matrix with corn starch or talc.
2. Put the top and bottom frame walls on the flange (the pins go into the holes), then slide the
   side walls in from above (tenons).
3. Lay a clay slab of about 15 mm and work it into the relief from the back with your fingers and
   a rolling pin. The back follows the front, giving an even wall thickness (important for firing).
4. Put a little extra clay on the two pad marks (top left/right) and press the back plate down
   onto the frame: it levels the rim and two solid, flat pads. Cut off the clay along the frame.
5. Leather-hard: push the hole punch through both guide bosses until the collar stops: two
   slanted hanging holes (Ø 8 mm, 40° upwards, 18 mm deep). Punch the tile number through the
   window. Remove the walls sideways (hexagon: the two halves up and down) and lift the tile.
6. Dry slowly under a cloth (1-2 weeks), then fire as high as the clay supplier specifies for
   frost resistance (garden ceramics: usually about 1000-1150 °C). Do not glaze.

## 3 · Concrete (stage 2, workshop)

Release agent: rapeseed oil, **no silicone spray**: it leaves a hydrophobic film (Mustafa et al.
2021). Brush surface retarder on the matrix, cast a fine-mortar facing (sand 0-2 mm), then tamp the
coarse backfill. Demould and wash out the retarded skin (Veeger et al. 2021). Wear gloves and goggles,
as fresh concrete is strongly alkaline. The concrete back plate with M6 sleeves follows in v1.

## 4 · Booster

Waste-paper pulp + **at least the same amount** of topsoil from a spot where moss already grows
(Jakubovskis 2025: "soil crust"), pressed into the moss nests. Small moss cushions can be pressed
into the paste: the steep lower edge of each nest holds them. Collect only small amounts, only from school grounds,
paving joints and walls, never in protected areas. Sphagnum and *Leucobryum glaucum* are protected.

## 5 · Hang & document

Hanging: two stainless pins (Ø 6 mm) on the wall adapter, tilted 40° upwards; the tile is slid
onto them. Trial wall: at least 50 mm vertical and 20 mm horizontal gap (drill grid 170 × 200 mm). Every wall
needs **REF-FLAT and REF-GEO**. Register each tile with its orientation, print the reference card,
and take a photo every month with the card in the frame.
